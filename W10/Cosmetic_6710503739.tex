\section{Deployment and Interpretation}

\textbf{Rule with the highest confidence}\\
Only 3 rules reach confidence 0.7. The highest is:

\begin{center}
\textbf{IF \pcode{Eye\_Enhancer} (mascara) \quad THEN \quad \pcode{Eye\_Palette} (eyeshadow palette)}
\end{center}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcl}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Value} & \textbf{Calculation} \\
\midrule
Support    & 0.321 & $321 / 1{,}000$ transactions \\
Confidence & 0.899 & $321 / 357$ mascara buyers \\
Lift       & 2.36  & $0.899 / 0.381$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Meaning}\\
32.1\% of all baskets contain both products. Of the customers who buy mascara, 89.9\% also buy an
eyeshadow palette — against 38.1\% for customers in general, so mascara buyers are 2.36$\times$
more likely than average to buy a palette. Lift $> 1$ confirms a real association, not coincidence.

\textbf{Marketing strategy}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Product bundle:} sell \pcode{Eye\_Enhancer + Eye\_Palette} together as an
  ``Eye Look Set''. One basket in three already contains both.
  \item \textbf{TikTok content:} eye-makeup tutorials that apply the palette and then the mascara,
  so one clip sells both products.
  \item \textbf{Promotional campaign:} recommend the palette whenever mascara is added to the cart,
  and retarget the 36 mascara buyers who did not buy one.
\end{itemize}
